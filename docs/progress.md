- Micro, sine to noise: an orthogonal generator (dsp/osc/ortho.h) on the main sine. The sine's quadrature pair modulated by two independent random signals of half-width W makes a band of noise from f - W to f + W, centred on the note whatever W; one amount (0..1, `ortho`) moves the random weight against the sine at constant power and W from 2 Hz to 12 kHz. Three kinds (`ortho_mode`): smooth (white noise in two one-poles, -3 dB at W), stepped (held values every 1/2W s) and sparse (impulses of random sign, at most a gain of 4 so a thin crackle stays quieter). Each key draws the same noise every time. At 0 it is the sine to the bit: no fingerprint changes. Tests: the sine itself at 0, the sine's level within 1 dB at every amount and kind (sparse: never above), 65 %% of the power within +-W for a 200 Hz half-width around 4 kHz as the two one-poles predict, the band centred, deterministic. - Morphing filter table (dsp/filter/morph): like a wavetable, but each frame is a filter, a cascade of up to four second-order sections given as poles (frequency relative to the cutoff, Q) and zeros (place, depth, or a peak: zeros on the pole, gain 1 away from it). The position interpolates the poles in octaves and Q, the zeros' place on a warped 0..1 line (zeros crossing the spectrum draw in toward the origin on the way, so a section never notches its own resonance); biquads computed per block, each section set to its gain at the pole, ramped across the block (stable: the ramp stays in the stability triangle). Drive between the sections (tanh, its ceiling relative to each section's gain, the level given back), an envelope follower pushing the position up (follow), and a feedback loop around the cascade as in a physical model: the output, saturated, back into the input, scaled by the cascade's highest gain so that 1 is just past where it rings by itself. Two tables: classic (24 dB low-pass, band-pass, high-pass, high-pass an octave up) and growl (a soft low-pass and three dark formant peaks, oo oh ah ee). In the filter slot (vfilter: types morph and growl, sharing the nonlinear filters' memory, 268 bytes) and in va's filter 1 (filter_mode morph / growl, filter_morph, filter_feedback, filter_follow; filter_character = drive). Demo: va_morph_growl (a talking bass, its mouth on an eighth-note LFO, macros Mouth, Grunt, Push, Cutoff, level even across their travel within 2 dB, loudness at the library's median). 18-31 ns per sample on the host (the 2x ladder: 28). No other fingerprint changes.
